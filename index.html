ABOUT THIS STUDY

Divine carbon atom is an interactive reconstruction inspired by your carbon-atom video, presentation, GIF and the article Spiritual Secrets in the Carbon Atom.

The symbolic construction

Four curves meet at a common centre and terminate at the vertices of a regular tetrahedron (109.47° between axes). The same curves and adapted translucent lobes are viewed from different angles. The Aum loop, swastika elbows and alpha-and-omega arches are projections of the same four spiral surface ribbons from the supplied video.

The four rounded balloons have straight tetrahedral centre axes and recall the sp³ hybrid-orbital basis used in tetrahedral bonding. Their shared surface definition keeps the red lines attached when the width or expression changes. The width of each ring and the angle of its ink trace were fitted jointly across the video frames. These surfaces and red curves are artistic geometry. They are not calculated electron orbitals or high-probability spiral paths.

The swastika is included in the traditional spiritual context of the supplied material. All three symbolic views are artistic interpretations of these illustrations.

The physical carbon atom

Neutral carbon has six electrons and the ground-state configuration 1s² 2s² 2p². Four identical sp³ lobes do not describe a free carbon atom in its ground state. The quantum tab samples the squared amplitudes of screened hydrogenic orbitals, with effective nuclear charges Zeff = 5.70 for 1s and 3.25 for 2s/2p (Slater screening). These are independent-electron approximations, not an exact six-electron solution.

The total view uses two 1s, two 2s and two spherically averaged 2p electron contributions, appropriate as an illustrative unpolarized density. Individual real p-orbital views show possible basis orientations, not three occupied p orbitals. The 2s radial node and p angular nodes are preserved. A dot is a probability sample, not a particle moving along a trajectory. The nucleus is drawn much larger than its physical scale; the isotope marker refers to carbon-12 (six protons, six neutrons).

Controls & sources

Drag to rotate; scroll or pinch to zoom; right-drag, Shift-drag, or two-finger drag to pan. Focus the canvas to use arrow keys and +/−. Keys 1–3 select views, Space pauses or plays the tour, and R recentres. “Save this angle” stores your chosen perspective for the selected symbol. “Restore defaults” clears custom angles and resets the shape.
